## D002 — 2026-10-06: common evaluation start in 2009

The source was available for all ten ETFs through 2026-10-05; the common OHLC start was 2007-05-30, determined by BIL. A full year of BIL warmup was unavailable at the start of 2008. Development was therefore assigned to 2009-2013, walk-forward evaluation to 2014-2022, and warmup to 2007-2008. Alternatives were a mid-2008 start or a spliced cash index. Whole years support the predetermined annual selection schedule; splicing changes the traded instrument. Much of the 2008 crisis is excluded from evaluation, even where shorter features might be available. The change followed availability checks before strategy results.

## D003 — 2026-10-06: historical independence is unestablished

Prior inspection of H1/H2 history could not be recalled with certainty. The 2023-2025 period is reserved for a single historical check; 2026 is reported separately. Neither is called an independent holdout. Assuming untouched history would overstate the evidence. Prospective testing begins only after an actual model freeze; its date is not invented in advance.

## D004 — 2026-10-06: exact signals and risk controls

H1 uses relative geometric wealth against BIL over `t-L...t-21`, divided by `sigma63`, with `L=126/252` and `K=3/4`. H2 uses the fixed parent `H1_252_3` and requires at least 4/6 or 5/6 positive full calendar months. A zero month is not positive; excluded weights remain in BIL. Common controls are 25%/50% caps, `cov126`, and a 10% volatility target that only scales risk downward. Weights are not redistributed after clipping. Renormalizing survivors to 100% was rejected because it conceals the filter's allocation cost and expands risk. The six configurations remain fixed.

## D005 — 2026-10-06: BIL and corporate actions

BIL is purchased and incurs expenses; free USD receives no synthetic BIL return. Features and the statistical reference use theoretical total return, labeled BIL-relative rather than a guaranteed risk-free rate. Yahoo actions omit payable dates. Actual issuer dates take precedence; otherwise use ex-date plus 10 calendar days, with ex+0/30 scenarios reported separately. Receivables enter accounting on ex-date; cash enters only on payment. `auto_adjust=False` does not establish as-traded units. The BIL 1:2 reverse split in 2017 and EEM 3:1 split in 2008 are mandatory N1 controls. Undisclosed immediate credit and double counting adjusted prices plus cash dividends were rejected.

## D006 — 2026-10-06: research execution model

Orders are sized at the close with USD 100,000 initial capital, integer new order quantities, a 1% reserve, and execution at the next open. Sales precede proportionally reduced purchase fills. Cash cannot be negative; residual USD earns 0%. Immediate use of sale proceeds is a disclosed settlement simplification. Base cost is 10 bps one-way for every ETF; the scenario grid is 0/10/20/50 bps by 1/2-session lag. Sizing ideal weights using the future open violates the information contract. The model does not promise auction prices or brokerage execution. Reserves of 0% and 2% are diagnostics, not tuning candidates.

## D007 — 2026-10-06: selection and economic relevance

`P_A1` is separate from the six signals, selects only among the four H1 configurations, and uses rolling five-year history with the last two years for validation. Utility is `U = 252*mean(excess) - 1.5*252*var(excess)`. The near-tie threshold is 0.001; ordering is fixed with the primary H1 first. The minimum economically interesting `DeltaU` is 0.01 per year. Selection by final-period Sharpe or a dynamic H2 parent was rejected because it changes the research question or introduces leakage. Adaptive selection is not treated as an independent trial without disclosure.

## D008 — 2026-10-06: statistics and hypothesis rejection

All six fixed `DeltaU` values are compared with their assigned competitors. The primary bootstrap block is 63 sessions, with 21/126 alternatives, 10,000 repetitions, and Holm adjustment at 5%. Regression alpha uses monthly HAC(3) and two compact specifications; the second includes four asset-class proxies. A conditional bootstrap of the selected NAV does not test the whole selection pipeline. Separate bootstrap selection frequencies for `P_A1` are diagnostic, not confirmatory p-values. A positive intercept alone does not establish new alpha. Short reserved history and prior familiarity limit inference.

## D013 — 2026-10-06: distribution amount tolerance

Tolerance is USD 0.0005 per share in as-traded units, multiplied by `max(1, future_split_factor)`. Yahoo rounds dividends to 0.001 in either as-traded units (BIL) or post-split units (EEM); the as-traded rounding bound therefore grows with the factor. The tolerance was chosen after observing BIL 2009 and EEM rounding discrepancies. Its justification is source precision, rather than the reconciliation outcome. Materiality in bps is reported by ticker and event so small discrepancies remain visible. Exact equality would label every rounding difference a defect; a relative tolerance could miss errors in small BIL payments. The tolerance did not change after run `20261006T122119-c6bfb5c69b`.

## D021 — 2026-10-07: protocol interpretations in the N2 execution engine

The protocol (RESEARCH_PROTOCOL.md, section 8, lines 118-126) fixes the execution order, the sizing formula, the fill coefficient and the cash tolerance, but leaves open details without which an engine cannot be written. Seventeen interpretations are recorded below. They are built into `src/alpha_lab/market.py`, `ledger.py` and `engine.py` and described in [EXECUTION_MODEL.md](EXECUTION_MODEL.md). Items 1-7 are named in the N2 specification (section 11); items 8-17 arose during module implementation and were accepted during implementation. The interpretations add no tunable parameters and do not change the protocol's formulas; if any of them is judged to be a new economic rule, it requires a protocol version (lines 126 and 196). The effect of the interpretations on results was not measured. No registered run on real data existed when this entry was written; it was executed later, and the results are in [docs/n2/N2_REPORT.md](docs/n2/N2_REPORT.md).

1. Order of events at the open: split, receivable accrual, crediting of payouts, sales, purchases. The protocol says that shares held until the ex-date earn the distribution entitlement, and a purchase at the ex-date open does not (line 42), and that events come before the open, then sales, then purchases (line 122). It does not specify the order of accrual and crediting, or the fate of the entitlement when shares are sold at the ex-date open. Accrual is placed before crediting and before trades: a sale at the ex-date open keeps the entitlement, a purchase does not create it, and a payout whose payment session equals the ex-date (scenario proxy +0) is credited at the open of that session. Cash credited at the open funds purchases at the same open, as sale proceeds do (line 122). Alternatives: crediting before accrual would not credit a +0 payout at the ex-date open, contrary to line 42; accrual after trades would give the entitlement to the buyer and remove it from the seller. Consequence: a +0 payout becomes cash in the same session and immediately funds purchases; this is a calculation simplification, not a model of a brokerage account.

2. Pending order at a split. The protocol requires consistent units for positions and unfilled orders, retention of a fractional residual until sale, and an integer new target (line 120), but does not say how to rescale an order quantity. The order is multiplied by the split ratio without rounding again, while target_qty and held_qty stay in decision-time units. Alternatives: rounding the order after rescaling, which would change the decision, or recomputing the target at the post-split price, which would use information after the decision close (line 120 forbids sizing by a not-yet-known Open). Consequence: after a split with a ratio below 1 (BIL, 2017, ratio 0.5) the position and the order may be fractional; the residual is sold by an order for its quantity, and the purchase of a fractional order is rounded down (item 10).

3. Two-session delay. The protocol permits execution one extra session later (line 173) and forbids sizing by a not-yet-known Open (line 120); it does not say what stays fixed between decision and execution. The order quantity is fixed at the close of the decision date and changes only through a split, while fill is computed from actual cash at the open of the execution session, after that session's credits and sales. Alternatives: recomputing the size at the close of the next session (this would shift the decision information, not the execution) or computing fill from cash at the decision date. Consequence: with lag = 2, prices and payouts have time to change the account, so a purchase may be filled incompletely or leave cash unused.

4. No trade threshold. The protocol introduces no minimum trade size or dead band, and a new economic rule requires a protocol version (line 126). Any nonzero difference between target and actual quantity creates an order. Alternative: a threshold in dollars or as a share of NAV so as not to trade small residuals; it would be another parameter with no basis in the protocol. Consequence: after position drift, orders for a single share with cost c are possible; turnover and costs are higher than they would be with a threshold.

5. Proxy scenario for the payment date. For payouts with no known date, the protocol provides a sensitivity of +0 and +30 calendar days, and actual dates take precedence (lines 42 and 174); it does not say how to recompute. The engine recomputes the crediting date only for rows with payable_basis `proxy_ex_plus_10_calendar_days`: the first XNYS session no earlier than ex-date + k calendar days. `actual` rows are not changed. No new vintage is created for this, and the basis is recorded as `proxy_ex_plus_<k>_calendar_days`. Alternatives: a separate vintage for each k (would require new reconciliation, QA and a separate decision under D020) or applying k to all payouts (contradicts line 174). Departure from the specification text: the specification (sections 2 and 5) refers to the first session of the common calendar, while the code uses the XNYS calendar, because normalize.py writes XNYS dates and a payment date may fall after the last bar; on the approved vintage the common calendar and XNYS coincide (4,869 of 4,869 sessions), so the result is the same. Consequence: the vintage has one proxy row (BIL, ex-date 2008-03-03), so sensitivity to k affects at most one payout, and its contribution was not measured; for k = 10 the recomputed date equals the vintage `payable_date`, which the loader checks.

6. Cash and quantities in float64. The protocol gives a tolerance of cash >= -1e-8 USD for the numerical residual (line 124) and does not specify how cash is stored. Cash and quantities are stored in float64 without rounding to cents; a value below -1e-8 after purchases raises `ValueError`. Alternatives: rounding every movement to the cent (needs a rounding rule for costs and receivables that the protocol lacks) or Decimal (a rounding rule is still needed and it adds no conformance to the protocol). Consequence: cash and NAV contain fractions of a cent, identities are checked with tolerances, and manual reconciliations are compared with abs=1e-9.

7. Guard for the closed period. The period from 2023-01-01 is closed until N6 (lines 137 and 143), but the protocol does not describe the prohibition mechanism. A run is rejected if end_session is later than 2022-12-30 (the last session of 2022) or the execution session of any decision is later than end_session. Alternatives: checking only decision dates (a decision on 2022-12-30 would execute at the 2023-01-03 open, that is, on closed data) or relying on run discipline. Consequence: by default the last decision falls on 2022-11-30; the guard applies to the run and does not restrict file reads, because the loader reads the vintage through 2026-10-05; only a separate decision can lift it.

8. Fill coefficient in the result. The protocol defines the coefficient itself (line 122) but does not require the engine to report it. `ledger.execute_orders` returns the pair (trades, buy_fill), decisions.csv stores buy_fill, and without purchases the value is 1.0. Alternative: reconstructing the coefficient from the purchased and ordered quantity, but after floor it cannot be recovered exactly. Consequence: buy_fill = 1.0 in a decision row does not mean that purchases occurred; their presence is read from orders.csv and trades.csv.

9. Default decision dates. The protocol defines t as the last regular session of the month (line 50) but says nothing about an incomplete month at the end of the window or a decision whose execution session falls outside the window. By default a decision is taken on the last XNYS session of each calendar month in the window, if its execution session (index + lag) exists and is no later than end_session. Alternatives: the last data session of each month (a window ending mid-month would produce a false monthly date) or decisions without execution in the window (an order would remain pending after end_session). Departure from the specification text: section 4 refers to the last session of the month in the common calendar, while the code uses XNYS; on the approved vintage the calendars coincide (4,869 of 4,869), so the result is the same. Consequence: for a window ending 2022-12-30 the last decision falls on 2022-11-30; changing the window shifts decisions only at its edges.

10. Fractional purchase after a split. After a split an order may be fractional, for example 456.5, while the protocol requires whole shares (line 120) and purchase of floor(fill × requested_quantity) with the cancellation reason retained (line 122). floor(fill × q) is bought. The reason `insufficient_cash` is set only if fewer than floor(q) shares were bought; otherwise status `partial` gets the reason `fractional_quantity`. Alternatives: writing `insufficient_cash` everywhere (this attributes a cash shortfall where cash was available) or buying the fractional part as well (contradicts whole shares). Consequence: an order with a fractional part receives `partial` even when fill = 1, and the position stays below target by the fractional part. The required amount R is computed from the full fractional q, so when cash lies between the actual spending and R, fill falls below 1 and a purchase of another ticker may shrink by one share. R computed from the full q follows the specification (section 6) literally; the effect was not measured.

11. Status on invariant violation. The specification (section 8) calls such a run `failed`. The engine ends it with status `invariants_failed`: the result is frozen in `data/runs/<run_id>` for inspection, and the names of failed checks go to quality_warnings. This follows N1, where audit writes `quality_failed`: a defect found by a check differs from an exception in code, and the files are needed for inspection. Alternatives: status `failed` without freezing the result (nothing to inspect) or a nonzero command-line exit code (audit also returns 0 on `quality_failed`). Consequence: `simulate` exits with code 0 on `invariants_failed` and prints the run directory, so a consumer must check `passed` in invariants.json or the status in the journal; code that looks only for `failed` in the journal would miss such a run.

12. Numbering in invariant_rotation. In the specification (section 10), w_i = (1 + (m + i) mod 3) / Σ, where i is the ticker's index in alphabetical order; the base of the index is not stated. Adopted: i is counted from zero over tickers in ASCII order, m = 12 × year + month of the decision date. Alternative: one-based numbering shifts the rotation by one step. Consequence: the choice does not affect the invariant checks, and the rotation still produces trades every month; using the month number within the year instead of m gives the same weights, because 12 is divisible by 3. The test `test_invariant_rotation_weights` fixes the numbering, and changing the formula requires a new provider version (currently 1, recorded in config.json).

13. Scope of the payable contract check. The specification (section 2) states the contract for rows with dividend > 0 and does not say which rows of the file to check. The loader checks the contract and numeric columns only on sessions of the common calendar, that is, from 2007-05-30: earlier rows in the SPY, EFA and other ticker files are not used by the engine. Alternative: checking all file rows, in which case a defect in history before the common start, which does not affect the account, would stop a run. Consequence: the loader asserts nothing about rows before 2007-05-30.

14. Overlapping decisions and provider weights. The specification forbids a new decision before the previous one executes (section 6) and requires weights for all ten tickers (section 3). The engine rejects a decision list before the loop starts if a decision index is less than the previous index plus lag, even when the previous decision created no orders. Weights are accepted only if the keys equal the market tickers, the values are int or float (not bool), are finite and not less than 0, and `math.fsum` does not exceed 1 + 1e-12. Alternatives: rejecting only when orders are pending (would miss an overlap in which there happen to be no orders) and zero weight for unnamed tickers (would hide a provider error). Consequence: monthly decisions with lag <= 2 do not overlap, and the check fires on explicit date lists; test providers name all tickers, with zero weight written explicitly; `np.float32` and `np.int64` values are rejected, while `np.float64` passes.

15. Rejections before a Run exists. The protocol requires every run, including failed ones, to be registered (section 14, line 192, and [experiments/README.md](experiments/README.md)). The journal is opened in `run_simulation`, Scenario validates the grid when it is created, and argparse parses arguments earlier. Therefore EXPERIMENT_LOG.jsonl does not receive Scenario values outside the grid, an unknown provider name (`KeyError` from Python; the command line restricts the choice through `choices`) or argument-parsing errors. Rejections inside a Run are journaled: the closed period, a different or external vintage, and input-data errors as `failed`, and invariant violations as `invariants_failed`. Alternative: recording every call, including argument typos, for which the grid check would have to move inside Run. Consequence: an attempt to run a scenario outside the grid (for example `--cost 0.003`) leaves no trace in the journal and creates no results, while an attempt to reach the closed period leaves a trace.

16. Units and format of output files. The specification (section 7) lists the output file columns without units or signs. order_qty is written with a sign (minus means a sale) in execution-session units, after rescaling for a split; filled_qty is the executed quantity, unsigned; target_qty, held_qty and close refer to the decision moment, in pre-split units. Floating-point numbers are written with the format %.10g, and config.json and invariants.json as canonical JSON without loss of precision. Alternatives: full repr precision (rounding tails remain in the low digits, and differences between files are harder to read) or order_qty in decision units (filled_qty and trades are in execution units, and rows would stop reconciling). Consequence: prices and amounts in the CSV are not equal to in-memory values bit for bit, and identities recomputed from the CSV hold to about 1e-4 USD; the invariant checks work on unrounded values with a tolerance of 1e-6.

17. Names of invariant checks. The specification (section 8) lists seven checks without names. Alternative: free-text report that cannot be checked by key. Consequence: the N2 report and consumers refer to these keys, and renaming a check changes invariants.json and these references.

## D022 — 2026-10-07: N2 deferred items closed and N3 benchmark interpretations

The N3 specification ([docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-07-n3-benchmarks-design.md](docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-07-n3-benchmarks-design.md)) closes five items deferred from N2 (items 1-5, amending D021 items 11, 13, 14 and 15) and fixes the details of RESEARCH_PROTOCOL.md sections 5, 6 and 11 that the protocol leaves open (items 6-16). They are built into `src/alpha_lab/market.py`, `engine.py`, `features.py`, `portfolio.py`, `benchmarks.py`, `metrics.py`, `report.py` and `__main__.py` and described in [EXECUTION_MODEL.md](EXECUTION_MODEL.md). The interpretations add no tunable parameters and do not change the protocol's formulas; if any of them is judged to be a new economic rule, it requires a protocol version (lines 126 and 196). They were recorded before any registered benchmark run; no benchmark result existed when this entry was written.

1. Exit code on failed checks (amends D021 item 11). D021 left `simulate` with exit code 0 on `invariants_failed`, as `audit` did on `quality_failed`. Both commands now print the output directory and then exit with code 3 when the run's terminal status is `invariants_failed` or `quality_failed`. The code is derived from the same value that sets the journal status: `passed` in invariants.json for `simulate`, `technical_pass` in quality.json for `audit`. `completed` exits with 0; exceptions keep Python's code 1 and argparse errors code 2. `report` has no such status: it completes or fails. Alternative: keeping code 0 and requiring consumers to inspect files, which lets a script treat a failed check as success. Consequence: the result of a failed run is still frozen and the directory is printed, so it can be inspected; a caller must read 3 as "run exists, checks failed".

2. Events outside the common calendar (amends D021 item 13). `market_from_frames` additionally rejects, with `ValueError` naming ticker and session, a row whose session is not in the common calendar, lies between the start of the calendar and the last common session, and has a finite `dividend > 0` or a finite `split_ratio != 1`. It also rejects an accepted payout whose payment session is not later than the last common session and is not a common session. Rows before the start remain unchecked, as in D021 item 13; rows after the last common session are outside every run, and a later payment session stays allowed (the payout remains `receivable`). Basis: a corporate action on a dropped session would be invisible to the account, contrary to lines 40-44. Alternative: checking all file rows, which D021 item 13 rejected because history before the common start does not affect the account. Consequence: the approved vintage still loads, because XNYS and the common calendar coincide (4,869 of 4,869 sessions).

3. Payment sessions after scenario recomputation. `engine.pay_map` recomputes proxy rows for `proxy_pay_days` = k (D021 item 5) and then applies the payment rule of item 2 to every row. A payment session not later than the last market session and not a market session raises `ValueError` inside the Run, so the run is journaled as `failed`. Alternative: checking only at load time, which cannot see a date that exists only for k = 0 or 30. Consequence: on the approved vintage k = 0, 10 and 30 all pass.

4. Numeric types in weights (amends D021 item 14). `normalize.finite_number` accepts any `numbers.Real` that is finite, including `np.int64`, `np.float32` and `np.float64`, and rejects `bool`, `np.bool_`, NaN and infinities. The engine weight check keeps its other conditions: keys equal to the market tickers, values not less than 0, `math.fsum` not above 1 + 1e-12. N1 callers pass values parsed from JSON and are unaffected. Alternative: keeping D021's rejection of NumPy scalars, which would reject weights computed with NumPy for no economic reason. Consequence: a `np.float32` value is widened to float64 with its representation error, so a weight vector that sums to 1 in float32 may exceed the 1 + 1e-12 bound and be rejected.

5. Initial cash. `RunConfig.__post_init__` rejects an `initial_cash` that is not a finite real number greater than 0 (`ValueError`), using the predicate of item 4. The rejection happens when the configuration is created, before a Run exists, and belongs to the category of D021 item 15, so it is not journaled. Reason: `provenance.canonical_bytes` forbids NaN and infinities, so a non-finite value cannot be written into a `started` record. Alternative: journaling the rejection, which would need an encoding for non-finite numbers. Consequence: a zero or negative value no longer reaches turnover (`ZeroDivisionError` cannot occur); the command line does not expose `initial_cash`.

6. Warm-up and first decision. A benchmark decision at t requires at least 253 consecutive common-calendar closes (252 returns) for all ten tickers up to t (line 52). The requirement applies to every benchmark, including B0 and REF_SPY, which do not use the values, so that all comparisons share the first eligible decision. The first decision date, the close of 2008-12-31, executed at the open of 2009-01-02, is set by line 135 and the run window; the 253-close warm-up only guarantees that every benchmark accepts it; the 2008-12-31 row of daily.csv is the starting observation (NAV 100000) and is not evaluated as a return. Alternative: no warm-up for B0 and REF_SPY, which would start them earlier than B1-B3 and mix different windows. Consequence: all five runs use `--start 2008-12-31 --end 2022-12-30`; the default rule gives monthly decisions through 2022-11-30 (D021 item 9).

7. REF_SPY. The protocol defines REF_SPY as a reference without risk controls; the specification makes it a single purchase. The provider has schedule `first_only`: `run_simulation` sets `decision_sessions = (start_session,)` before the Run is opened, so the single decision appears in the journaled configuration. SPY = 1, all other weights 0, no caps and no volatility target; distributions stay USD cash and are not reinvested; there is no later trade. Alternative: monthly rebalancing, which would reinvest distributions and is the behavior of B0. Consequence: REF_SPY drifts with the account and its cash share grows by distributions; B0 reinvests free cash at each monthly decision through ordinary rebalancing.

8. B3 selection. A ticker is selected when (T_i,t / T_i,t-252) / (T_BIL,t / T_BIL,t-252) - 1 is strictly greater than 0, with t-252 the session 252 sessions before t and T the theoretical total return index (line 40); there is no 21-session skip. The weights use K = 9, so q carries the factor m/9 with m the number of selected tickers, and the remainder stays in BIL (line 64). An empty selection gives BIL = 1 with a = 1 (V = 0, line 66). Alternative: K = m, which would invest fully in the selected tickers regardless of how few pass. Consequence: B3 holds less risk when few tickers pass the trend filter, and an empty selection coincides with B0 in weights.

9. Covariance and volatility target. The 9 × 9 covariance matrix of 126 daily excess returns (annualized by 252) is checked in full: asymmetry above 1e-12 or a smallest eigenvalue below -1e-12 raises `ValueError`. In `common_risk`, V² = v'Σv below -1e-12 raises `ValueError`, V² in [-1e-12, 0) is set to 0, and a = 1 when V = 0, otherwise min(1, 0.10 / V) (lines 68-71). The BIL weight is 1 - fsum of the other weights; a value in [-1e-12, 0) is set to 0 and a lower value raises `ValueError`. Alternative: clamping negative eigenvalues, which would hide an input defect. Consequence: a failed check stops the run (`failed`) rather than producing a silently adjusted portfolio.

10. Cash return. r_BIL_TR in e_d = r_d - r_BIL_TR,d comes from the theoretical total return index of BIL, not from B0's NAV (line 46). Alternative: B0's NAV, which includes costs, the 1% reserve, whole-share rounding and the timing of reinvestment, and would make the excess return of B0 differ from zero for reasons unrelated to cash. Consequence: the `mean_excess` and `sharpe_bil` of B0 measure the implementation gap to the theoretical cash return.

11. Turnover denominator. The protocol defines one-way turnover as Σ|trade notional| / pretrade_NAV without defining the moment (line 157). The denominator is NAV at the close of the decision session, the NAV from which orders are sized. Alternative: NAV at the open of the execution session, which would use a value that differs from the sizing NAV by one overnight move. Consequence: turnover equals the `turnover` column of decisions.csv; it is not divided by 2.

12. Period base and attribution. A period's returns are those of its sessions within the run, and its base NAV is the NAV at the close of the last run session before it, so the first return of 2009 includes the entry relative to initial NAV (line 157). Decisions, turnover, costs, cost_ratio and mean_target_risky are counted by execution session, not by decision session: a decision on 2008-12-31 belongs to 2009. Alternative: attribution by decision date, which would place the first entry in 2008, outside every published period. Consequence: periods that begin on 1 January do not lose the entry; periods are labelled through 2022-12-31, and `metrics.py` raises `ValueError` if any daily session is later than 2022-12-30.

13. Cost ratio. `cost_ratio` is the sum over the period's decisions of costs_usd / NAV at the decision close (line 157: costs as a fraction of NAV). It is a sum of relative costs, not the exact loss of compound return. Alternative: costs divided by the average NAV, or the difference between gross and net compound returns, which needs a second simulation without costs. Consequence: the value approximates the cost drag and is meant for comparing benchmarks.

14. Undefined statistics. `sharpe_bil` is `null` when the sample standard deviation of e is exactly 0 (line 157: undefined, not infinity). Numbers that are undefined are `null`, never NaN or infinity. A period without returns is omitted from `metrics.json`. Alternative: a sentinel value, which would be read as a result. Consequence: consumers must handle `null`.

15. Benchmark run files and journal. A run of a `benchmark` provider records the purpose `N3 benchmark run` and `candidate_ids` = [provider name] in both the `started` and the terminal record, and freezes nine files: the seven N2 files plus weights.csv (one row per decision: `decision_session`, `w_<TICKER>` for ten tickers in ASCII order, `usd` = 1 - fsum of the weights) and metrics.json (canonical JSON, `schema_version` 1, computed in memory from unrounded values, never from the CSV files). Runs of `test` providers keep the purpose `N2 execution run`, an empty candidate list and seven files. Alternative: a separate command for benchmarks, which would duplicate the run, journal and freeze logic. Consequence: the provider registry carries `schedule` and `kind`, and the report identifies a benchmark run by its frozen provider.

16. Report. `python -m alpha_lab report --runs DIR...` is a Run with purpose `N3 benchmark report` and `candidate_ids` B0, B1, B2, B3, REF_SPY. It fails with `ValueError` unless exactly five directories in `data/runs` hold the five benchmarks once each, pass `provenance.verify`, contain the nine files, have one `started` and one `completed` record on a clean tree with a non-null `git_sha`, whose `data_sha256`, `output_paths` and `config_sha256` agree with the directory and `config.json`, have `invariants.json` with `passed` true, and share the approved manifest hash, scenario, window and initial cash, with current provider versions and metrics schema. Code and environment identity is also required: each `started` record has the purpose `N3 benchmark run`, `candidate_ids` = [provider name] and an `environment_manifest_sha256` equal to that of the report run, and the `src` tree of its `git_sha` (`git rev-parse <sha>:src`) equals the `src` tree of the report run's own `git_sha`; a mismatch names the run. Its outputs `benchmarks.json` and `benchmarks.md` contain no run id, timestamp or path; the run ids and run manifest hashes go to the report manifest metadata. A repeated run and a repeated report are compared by the manifest `files` dictionaries (per-file SHA-256), not by manifest bytes, which contain run identifiers. The `--expected-sha256` option exists for tests on synthetic vintages; a registered report must journal the approved hash `f89346107cf7da6ca052693d188b8a576a08d42024c86865b0a42a63b1d294f2`. Alternative: comparing manifest bytes, which can never be equal for two runs. Consequence: a registered report cannot be produced from runs of another vintage without a new decision.

## D023 — 2026-10-08: N4 interpretations, viewing restriction and run procedure

The N4 specification ([docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-08-n4-hypotheses-design.md](docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-08-n4-hypotheses-design.md)) fixes the details of RESEARCH_PROTOCOL.md sections 5, 7, 9, 11 and 13 that the protocol leaves open for the six configurations H1_252_3, H1_252_4, H1_126_3, H1_126_4, H2_4of6 and H2_5of6, supplements D022 item 11 (item 1), and defines the procedure for the registered runs and the status of the configurations. The decisions are built into `src/alpha_lab/features.py`, `portfolio.py`, `hypotheses.py`, `engine.py`, `report.py`, `hypothesis_report.py` and `__main__.py` and described in [EXECUTION_MODEL.md](EXECUTION_MODEL.md) and [experiments/README.md](experiments/README.md). Items 1, 3, 6, 7 and 10 were amended on 8 October 2026, also before any registered H1/H2 run: item 1 in wording only; the report checks of item 7 were strengthened (all seven invariant flags, capped weights recomputed from `q`, turnover and `buy_fill` bounds, a clean report tree), the withholding of item 6 was extended to serialization and freezing, and its excluded list and attribution rules were clarified (cash shares are excluded; `scale` < 1 and `buy_fill` < 1 are evaluated on the values parsed from the frozen `%.10g` text), the scope of the real-vintage tests in item 10 was stated separately for H1/H2 and the benchmarks, and a known limitation of the warm-up check was added to item 3; the earlier text is in the history of this file at commit `54100ac`.

1. Turnover denominator (supplements D022 item 11). Protocol line 157 defines one-way turnover as Σ|trade notional| / pretrade_NAV without fixing the moment. This item adds the size of the difference for B3 and states the reading: in this project `pretrade_NAV` means NAV at the decision close. The recomputation is `docs/n4/turnover_denominator.py` (commit 3256697), a read-only diagnostic outside `src` that is not journaled. Command, run from the project root: `PYTHONPATH=src .venv/Scripts/python docs/n4/turnover_denominator.py data/runs/20261007T180033-bba99392df data/derived/20261006T172442-80ef993493` (on the N4 branch the same command was run against the run directory and vintage of the project root, `../../data/runs/...`). Alternative: replacing the denominator, which would change the published N3 tables and the definition on which D022 item 13 and the engine rest. Consequence: the protocol stays at version 1.0, because line 157 does not fix the moment, the decision-close NAV is also a pre-trade NAV and the sizing NAV, turnover changes no order, trade or NAV, and no section 13 criterion uses turnover. 2. Proxy payment lag wording. docs/n3/N3_REPORT.md said "proxy payment lag 10 sessions". The contract (protocol line 42, D021 item 5) is 10 calendar days, then the first eligible session no earlier than ex-date + 10 calendar days. The report now says so, and a dated section `Corrections` at its end records this change together with the turnover wording of item 1. 3. H1 window indexing and tie-break. At the close of t, the momentum of a risky ticker is M_i = (T_i[t-21] / T_i[t-L]) / (T_BIL[t-21] / T_BIL[t-L]) - 1 (line 95), with T the theoretical total return index and positional indices `T[t-21] = index.iloc[-22]` and `T[t-L] = index.iloc[-(L+1)]`. The window therefore holds exactly L - 21 daily returns: 231 for L = 252 and 105 for L = 126 (line 93). A history with fewer than L + 1 sessions raises `ValueError`; the common warm-up of 253 closes (D022 item 6) applies to every provider. Known limitation: `require_warmup` counts history rows, not XNYS sessions, so a vintage with a missing interior session would pass the warm-up and shift the positional windows; the approved vintage holds every XNYS session from 2007-05-30 to 2026-10-05 (4869 of 4869, checked on 8 October 2026 against `normalize.calendar`), and the check is not changed in N4. Sigma is measured through t (line 99). The score is S_i = M_i / sigma_i (line 97). A ticker is eligible when S_i > 0, strictly. Eligible tickers are ranked by the key (-S_i, ticker) with the ticker in ASCII order, so the ticker breaks only exact ties (line 99). In `signals.csv` the rank is 1-based among eligible tickers and empty for a ticker that is not eligible; selected means rank <= K. The first min(K, number eligible) tickers are selected and missing slots are not filled with non-positive scores. `inverse_vol` is called with k = K, so q carries the factor m/K and unused slots stay in BIL (line 66); an empty selection gives BIL = 1. Alternative: filling missing slots with the best non-positive scores, which would hold tickers whose trailing excess return is not positive and contradicts the trend filter of line 97. Consequence: H1 holds less risk when few tickers pass, as B3 does (D022 item 8); the configurations with K = 3 and K = 4 and the same lookback share momentum, score, eligibility and rank, and the selected set for K = 3 is a subset of that for K = 4.

4. H2 month-end levels, filter and BIL convention. H2 computes its parent H1(252, 3) on the same history with the same code (line 101). `month_end_levels` takes the last seven month-end sessions of the history and the index rows at them; the month of t contributes t, because its last session is complete at the 18:00 decision time (line 107). It raises `ValueError`, and the run stops (line 54), when fewer than seven months are present, when the months are not consecutive calendar months, or when any of the seven sessions is not the last XNYS session of its calendar month (a per-year cached XNYS lookup); an incomplete month is never skipped, so a t that is not the last XNYS session of its month raises. The excess return of month j is E_i,j = (T_i,end(j) / T_i,end(j-1)) / (T_BIL,end(j) / T_BIL,end(j-1)) - 1 (line 103), j = 1 oldest to 6 newest. A month counts as positive when E_i,j > 0; E = 0 does not count (line 107). The filter passes when the positive-month count is at least h. The H2 weight of a ticker is the parent's weight when the filter passes and an exact 0.0 otherwise, as a copy with no arithmetic on the weight. The BIL weight is 1 - fsum of the remaining risky weights (the convention of line 71 and D022 item 9: a value in [-1e-12, 0) is set to 0, a lower value raises `ValueError`). There is no reranking, no replacement ticker, no redistribution, no second `common_risk` and no upward rescaling (line 107). H2 rows in `signals.csv` carry the parent's H1 columns except `weight`, and add `parent_weight`, `excess_1` to `excess_6` (oldest to newest), `positive_months` and `filter_pass`. Alternative: leaving released weight in USD, or re-running the volatility target on the filtered portfolio; the first would break the BIL convention of line 71, the second would raise other weights contrary to line 107. Consequence: the H2 BIL weight is never lower than the parent's, and H2 never holds a ticker the parent did not select.

## D024 — 2026-10-08: post-run N4 numerical validation correction

## D025 — 2026-10-09: N5 interpretations, evaluation procedure and freeze

The N5 specification ([docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-09-n5-evaluation-design.md](docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-09-n5-evaluation-design.md)) was approved by the project owner on 9 October 2026, and the corrections of its sections 15 and 16 were acknowledged the same day. This entry records the interpretations that RESEARCH_PROTOCOL.md sections 9 to 13 leave open for the walk-forward evaluation, the adaptive policy P_A1 and the statistics (items 1 to 14, numbered P1 to P14 in the specification), and the procedure around them (items 15 to 20). It was written before any N5 code, run or result: no H1/H2 return, risk or utility figure had been computed or shown, and the viewing restriction of D023 item 6 still applied. The interpretations add no tunable parameter and do not change a protocol formula; if any of them is judged to be a new economic rule, it requires a protocol version (lines 126 and 196). The specification is the detailed source; where this entry is shorter, the specification governs.

1. Statistics basis (P1). The walk-forward statistics use the slice 2014-01-02 to 2022-12-30 of the continuous accounts that start at 2008-12-31, the form already published for B0-B3 in the N3 report (`walk_forward` block, D022 item 12). The sample is 108 months and 2,266 daily returns. Alternative: fresh accounts that start in cash at 2013-12-31 for every candidate, which would change the published N3 `walk_forward` figures and need a second benchmark set. Consequence: the accounts of the six configurations and of B0-B3 enter 2014 with positions carried from earlier years, as in N3.

2. P_A1 comparator (P2). P_A1 must start in cash (it needs five prior years). The comparison "fixed H1_252_3 alongside" (line 147) uses one supplementary run of the provider H1_252_3 over the P_A1 window (2013-12-31 to 2022-12-30, in cash), the P_A1 comparator. It is not a new candidate. Alternative: the slice of the continuous account, which would differ from P_A1 by an entry cost and one overnight move. Consequence: thirteen registered runs instead of twelve.

3. Period lists (P3). P_A1 and its comparator are evaluated on the walk-forward list only: `walk_forward`, `2014-2016`, `2017-2019`, `2020-2022` and the years 2014 to 2022. `full`, `development` and 2009 to 2013 are not computed for them, because `full` would silently mean 2014-2022. Alternative: the full list with empty periods omitted. Consequence: no figure for P_A1 is labeled with a period it does not cover.

4. Year of a decision (P4). A decision under P_A1 uses the selection of the calendar year of its execution session; the December decision therefore uses the next year's selection, made at the same close (line 153). Alternative: the year of the decision session. Consequence: the January order of year Y carries the choice made for Y.

5. Validation segment (P5). For selection year Y the segment runs from the last XNYS session of December Y-3 to the last XNYS session of December Y-1. The account starts in cash at the first, uses the main scenario, and has the default monthly decisions whose execution is inside the segment (24 decisions, December Y-3 to November Y-1). U is computed from the daily excess returns over the theoretical BIL return, the first return including the entry. The three earlier years are history only. Alternative: a segment with a final-month decision whose execution lies outside it. Consequence: for 2014 the validation window 2012-2013 lies inside the development period, and the selection log shows candidate utilities for windows from 2012 to 2021.

6. Close candidates (P6). With best_U the maximum, the close set holds every candidate with U >= best_U - 0.001 (floating-point comparison as written); the first member in the order H1_252_3, H1_252_4, H1_126_3, H1_126_4 is selected (line 151). Alternative: none; the protocol states the rule. Consequence: the preference favors the original configuration, a longer window and a smaller set, and is not an out-of-sample ranking.

7. Fallback and defects (P7). The fallback to H1_252_3, with a warning in the selection log and in the journal `quality_warnings`, applies only when a U is not finite while the data and the checks are complete. A missing session, a failed warm-up, an exception in a provider or a failed invariant in a validation account stops the run. The same rule, including the fallback, is applied to each stability replicate (line 167: "under the same rule"), and replicates that use the fallback are counted separately. Alternative: fallback for every failure, which would hide defects. Consequence: a defect stops the campaign instead of producing a silently adjusted selection.

8. Bootstrap random numbers (P8). One `numpy.random.Generator(PCG64(seed))` is created afresh for each (n, L) pair; block starts are drawn with `integers(0, n, size=(B, ceil(n / L)))`, indices are taken modulo n, and each replicate is truncated to n observations. Seeds: 20261006 for the paired utility bootstrap (B = 10000, L in {21, 63, 126}) and 20261007 for the P_A1 stability bootstrap (B = 1000, L = 63). Alternative: one generator for the whole report, which would make results depend on the order of tests. Consequence: all tests with the same n and L share their replicates, and the result does not depend on the chunk size or the order.

9. Regression intervals (P9). Newey-West covariance with the Bartlett kernel, lag 3, and the finite-sample factor n / (n - k), where k counts every coefficient including the intercept. The interval is the estimate plus or minus 1.96 standard errors. Annual alpha is 12 times the monthly intercept; its standard error and both interval endpoints are scaled by 12. Alternative: Student t with n - k degrees of freedom, which needs SciPy or a hand-coded quantile; with 108 months the difference is small. Consequence: the intervals are asymptotic and are labeled so. No alpha figure is formed when n < 36, when the design matrix is rank deficient or when its condition number exceeds 1e8 (line 165).

10. Monthly returns and class proxies (P10). The account monthly return is NAV at the last session of the month over NAV at the last session of the previous month, minus one; the BIL return is the same from the theoretical total return index. A class proxy is the unweighted mean of the monthly excess returns over BIL of the members of the group in `portfolio.GROUPS` (Equity: SPY, EFA, EEM; Treasury: IEF, TLT; Credit: LQD, HYG; Real: GLD, DBC). Alternative: none proposed; line 165 forbids optimizing weights. Consequence: the proxies are exposure controls, not tradable benchmarks or academic factors.

11. DSR and PBO (P11). Not implemented in N5. A DSR needs an explicit trial set, and the owner has chosen not to quantify prior views and reviews (item 18). Alternative: implement with the six configurations plus P_A1 as trials, which would present a trial count that does not reflect the actual history. Consequence: the multiple-testing caveat stays unresolved in size in every N5 document.

12. Journal purposes (P12). N5 runs record the purposes `N5 benchmark run`, `N5 hypothesis run` and `N5 policy run`, and the report `N5 evaluation report`, so that the N3 and N4 reports accept and reject exactly what they accepted and rejected before. Alternative: reuse the old purposes, which would let the N4 report accept runs with a different file set. Consequence: the run files are nine for a benchmark run, ten for an N5 hypothesis run (the nine files of an N4 run plus `metrics.json`) and eleven for the policy run (plus `selection.json`).

13. P_A1 inference (P13). The utility difference of P_A1 against its comparator is reported with the three basic intervals and no p-value, labeled conditional on the realized selections (line 167). Alternative: also report a p-value, which would need a label that keeps it from being read as a confirmatory claim. Consequence: P_A1 is outside the Holm family.

14. Where the tests are computed (P14). The formal tests, the bootstrap, the Holm adjustment and the regressions are computed for the walk-forward period only. The development and full periods receive the descriptive metrics without interval or test, because the protocol names the walk-forward and reserved periods for the regressions (line 165) and for the decision criteria (line 186) and names the development period for no test. This is an interpretation of the staged evaluation. Alternative: tests on all three periods, which adds comparisons on the same data without a protocol basis. Consequence: the reserved period and the section 13 decisions are N6.

15. Selection computation and its checks. The P_A1 selection for year Y is computed on the history truncated to s_Y, the last session of December Y-1, whenever it is first requested, and is identical whatever decision requests it. It may be cached only inside one provider instance created for one simulation, with a key that includes the year, the vintage manifest hash and s_Y; there is no module-level cache. The provider rejects a decision whose selection year is outside 2014 to 2022. The evaluation report checks the semantics of the selection log from the logged utilities (segment boundaries, the maximum, the close set, the preference order, the year mapping of item 4); the recomputation of the utilities is an independent test on the real vintage. Alternative: a cache keyed by year only, which could return a selection computed from another history. Consequence: the 2023 selection is not computed in N5; its first computation is an N6 act under the freeze of protocol section 13.

16. Viewing and freeze procedure. D023 item 6 still applies to the real N4 run directories and to every H1/H2 output until the N5 code is frozen. Development uses synthetic markets, and real-vintage tests print only counts, flags and maximum differences. The freeze requires the full test suite to pass on a clean tree, a review of the whole branch with no critical or important defect, and the commit, its `src` tree hash and the test result to be recorded in STATUS.md. The registered campaign then runs once; during it the failure messages of hypothesis and policy runs, and the errors of the evaluation report, stay value-free (rule, run and location, never a value or a traceback containing one). The first completed evaluation report is the first time a figure is shown; D023 item 6 is superseded for N5 outputs from that moment, and its text is preserved. A change of rule, candidate, window or period after seeing the figures is a new attempt with its own decision record. A bug fix follows D023 item 8: all thirteen runs are rerun on the new tree with parent links, followed by a new report, and every attempt is disclosed. Statistics read the frozen `daily.csv`, which carries 10 significant digits (D021 item 16); `metrics.json` is computed from unrounded values, so their comparison tolerance is 1e-7. Alternative: lifting the restriction at the start of N5, which would let the figures shape the code before it is frozen. Consequence: the reserved period stays closed and the N5 report states what was shown and when.

Thirteen runs (B0, B1, B2, B3, REF_SPY, the six configurations, P_A1 and its comparator) on the approved vintage and the main scenario; the evaluation report; a repeat of each run with `--parent`, then a repeat of the report; and a comparison of the manifest `files` dictionaries for all fourteen pairs. ## D026 — 2026-10-10: independent position-balance audit of frozen runs

The seven run invariants of `engine.run_invariants` were written in D021 and checked on every run since. During the review of the N5 branch it was found that none of them reconciles a recorded position with its split events and filled trades. This entry records the audit that closes the gap. It was decided after the N5 figures were published (D025, addition of 10 October 2026) and is recorded here before its first execution.

1. The gap. The nav identity, the cash-flow identity and the split check test quantities against each other, but each of them takes the recorded share quantities as given: `split_quantity_only` compares the position and the order quantity across a split, and no invariant compares the change in a position from one session to the next with the filled trades of that session. An erroneous change of share quantities that is also reflected in positions value and NAV can therefore pass all seven. This was demonstrated on a synthetic counterexample: in a test run, one share is added to a position from one session on, with positions value and NAV moved by that share's close, and all seven invariants stay true. The gap is in correctness control. It is not evidence that any existing result is wrong, and this entry does not claim that one is. Alternative: rely on the seven invariants as registered. Rejected: a share created or lost without a trade would change NAV, and with it a performance conclusion, without being detected. Consequence: "all seven invariants passed" is not read as proof that the recorded positions follow from the recorded trades and splits.

2. The check. For every run, every ETF `i` and every session `t` of the run window, in order, the expected position is H_expected[i, t] = s[i, t] × H_recorded[i, t-1] + Σ filled buy quantity of `i` at `t` − Σ filled sell quantity of `i` at `t`, where H_recorded is `qty_<TICKER>` of `daily.csv`, `s` is the split ratio of the vintage (1 when there is none) and the filled quantities come from `trades.csv`. The traded quantities are already in post-split units of session `t`, because splits are applied before execution (EXECUTION_MODEL.md, section 3). At the first session of the window the previous position is zero, because accounts start in cash. Two variants are computed: per session from the recorded previous state, and cumulatively from zero using only the split ratios and the trades, which also detects a drift too small for the per-session comparison. `daily.csv` carries 10 significant digits, so a comparison uses a tolerance of 1e-9 absolute plus 1e-9 of the sum of the operand magnitudes, which is twice the largest rounding error of those operands; a one-share error exceeds it in every session for positions below about 5e8 shares. The trade checks are: the price equals the vintage open of that session and ticker; the notional equals quantity × price; the cost equals the scenario cost × notional; each trade links to exactly one row of `orders.csv` with the same execution session and ticker, a filled quantity equal to the trade quantity and an order quantity of the same sign as the side; every order with a positive filled quantity has exactly one trade and every order with a filled quantity of zero has none. The sessions of `daily.csv` must equal the vintage sessions of the run window. Alternative: compare only the per-session variant. Rejected: a drift below the tolerance in every session would never be seen. Consequence: the tolerance leaves a margin of 2x for positions and about 1.33x for the notional product against the rounding of the stored files.

3. The decision. The audit is a read-only program over frozen files, `python -m alpha_lab position-audit`, applied to every completed run of the journal: the N2 execution runs, the N3 benchmark runs, the N4 hypothesis runs and the N5 runs, 62 runs. It reads only `config.json`, `daily.csv`, `trades.csv` and `orders.csv` of each run and the approved vintage, and does not use the engine's account object. It is a journaled attempt with the purpose `Position audit` and no candidate (it evaluates none), it verifies each run directory with `provenance.verify`, and it freezes `position_audit.json` in `data/reports/<run_id>/`. The journal status is `completed` when every run passes and otherwise the terminal status `audit_failed`, with the failing run ids as quality warnings; a failure stays in the journal. The command exits with 0 when every run passes and with 3 when a run fails. Alternative: an eighth engine invariant. Rejected for now: it would change `invariants.json` of every run, so the N3, N4 and N5 validators, which read that file, would need versioned validators, and N3, N4 and N5 would need full, disclosed reruns (D023, item 8; D025, item 16) for a control that a read-only audit provides without touching them. Consequence: the audit can be repeated at any time on frozen runs without a rerun.

4. Consequences. The engine, the run outputs, the experiment journal records of the audited runs and the N3, N4 and N5 validators are unchanged, and no run is repeated. The audit is not tamper protection: a run altered together with all of its records, consistently, would pass. It does not check the journal records of the audited runs; its receipt records the manifest hash of each audited run, so the audited bytes are identified. A failing run suspends the economic conclusions that depend on it, as the first sentence of RESEARCH_PROTOCOL.md section 13 (line 184) provides for causality or accounting errors, until a decision records the cause and the response. A pass establishes the reconciliation of positions and trades only, not the correctness of signals, prices or costs beyond the checks listed. The registered audit has not yet been run; its result will be added to this entry after execution.

5. Status of the decision. The decision was made after the N5 figures were published. It changes no rule, candidate, parameter, window, period, statistic or status of D025, and it neither repeats nor replaces an N5 attempt, so it is not a new N5 attempt. The audit may add information about the correctness of past runs; it cannot add a favorable result for any hypothesis.
