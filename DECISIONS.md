## D002 — 2026-10-06: common evaluation start in 2009

The source was available for all ten ETFs through 2026-10-05; the common OHLC start was 2007-05-30, determined by BIL. A full year of BIL warmup was unavailable at the start of 2008. Development was therefore assigned to 2009-2013, walk-forward evaluation to 2014-2022, and warmup to 2007-2008. Alternatives were a mid-2008 start or a spliced cash index. Whole years support the predetermined annual selection schedule; splicing changes the traded instrument. Much of the 2008 crisis is excluded from evaluation, even where shorter features might be available. The change followed availability checks before strategy results.

## D003 — 2026-10-06: historical independence is unestablished

Prior inspection of H1/H2 history could not be recalled with certainty. The 2023-2025 period is reserved for a single historical check; 2026 is reported separately. Neither is called an independent holdout. Assuming untouched history would overstate the evidence. Prospective testing begins only after an actual model freeze; its date is not invented in advance.

## D004 — 2026-10-06: exact signals and risk controls

H1 uses relative geometric wealth against BIL over `t-L...t-21`, divided by `sigma63`, with `L=126/252` and `K=3/4`. H2 uses the fixed parent `H1_252_3` and requires at least 4/6 or 5/6 positive full calendar months. A zero month is not positive; excluded weights remain in BIL. Common controls are 25%/50% caps, `cov126`, and a 10% volatility target that only scales risk downward. Weights are not redistributed after clipping. Renormalizing survivors to 100% was rejected because it conceals the filter's allocation cost and expands risk. The six configurations remain fixed.

## D005 — 2026-10-06: BIL and corporate actions

BIL is purchased and incurs expenses; free USD receives no synthetic BIL return. Features and the statistical reference use theoretical total return, labeled BIL-relative rather than a guaranteed risk-free rate. Yahoo actions omit payable dates. Actual issuer dates take precedence; otherwise use ex-date plus 10 calendar days, with ex+0/30 scenarios reported separately. Receivables enter accounting on ex-date; cash enters only on payment. `auto_adjust=False` does not establish as-traded units. The BIL 1:2 reverse split in 2017 and EEM 3:1 split in 2008 are mandatory N1 controls. Undisclosed immediate credit and double counting adjusted prices plus cash dividends were rejected.

## D006 — 2026-10-06: research execution model

Orders are sized at the close with USD 100,000 initial capital, integer new order quantities, a 1% reserve, and execution at the next open. Sales precede proportionally reduced purchase fills. Cash cannot be negative; residual USD earns 0%. Immediate use of sale proceeds is a disclosed settlement simplification. Base cost is 10 bps one-way for every ETF; the scenario grid is 0/10/20/50 bps by 1/2-session lag. Sizing ideal weights using the future open violates the information contract. The model does not promise auction prices or brokerage execution. Reserves of 0% and 2% are diagnostics, not tuning candidates.

## D007 — 2026-10-06: selection and economic relevance

`P_A1` is separate from the six signals, selects only among the four H1 configurations, and uses rolling five-year history with the last two years for validation. Utility is `U = 252*mean(excess) - 1.5*252*var(excess)`. The near-tie threshold is 0.001; ordering is fixed with the primary H1 first. The minimum economically interesting `DeltaU` is 0.01 per year. Selection by final-period Sharpe or a dynamic H2 parent was rejected because it changes the research question or introduces leakage. Adaptive selection is not treated as an independent trial without disclosure.

## D008 — 2026-10-06: statistics and hypothesis rejection

All six fixed `DeltaU` values are compared with their assigned competitors. The primary bootstrap block is 63 sessions, with 21/126 alternatives, 10,000 repetitions, and Holm adjustment at 5%. Regression alpha uses monthly HAC(3) and two compact specifications; the second includes four asset-class proxies. A conditional bootstrap of the selected NAV does not test the whole selection pipeline. Separate bootstrap selection frequencies for `P_A1` are diagnostic, not confirmatory p-values. A positive intercept alone does not establish new alpha. Short reserved history and prior familiarity limit inference.

## D013 — 2026-10-06: distribution amount tolerance

Tolerance is USD 0.0005 per share in as-traded units, multiplied by `max(1, future_split_factor)`. Yahoo rounds dividends to 0.001 in either as-traded units (BIL) or post-split units (EEM); the as-traded rounding bound therefore grows with the factor. The tolerance was chosen after observing BIL 2009 and EEM rounding discrepancies. Its justification is source precision, rather than the reconciliation outcome. Materiality in bps is reported by ticker and event so small discrepancies remain visible. Exact equality would label every rounding difference a defect; a relative tolerance could miss errors in small BIL payments. The tolerance did not change after run `20261006T122119-c6bfb5c69b`.

## D021 — 2026-10-07: protocol interpretations in the N2 execution engine

The protocol (RESEARCH_PROTOCOL.md, section 8, lines 118-126) fixes the execution order, the sizing formula, the fill coefficient and the cash tolerance, but leaves open details without which an engine cannot be written. Seventeen interpretations are recorded below. They are built into `src/alpha_lab/market.py`, `ledger.py` and `engine.py` and described in [EXECUTION_MODEL.md](EXECUTION_MODEL.md). Items 1-7 are named in the N2 specification (section 11); items 8-17 arose during module implementation and were accepted during implementation. The interpretations add no tunable parameters and do not change the protocol's formulas; if any of them is judged to be a new economic rule, it requires a protocol version (lines 126 and 196). The effect of the interpretations on results was not measured. No registered run on real data existed when this entry was written; it was executed later, and the results are in [docs/n2/N2_REPORT.md](docs/n2/N2_REPORT.md).

1. Order of events at the open: split, receivable accrual, crediting of payouts, sales, purchases. The protocol says that shares held until the ex-date earn the distribution entitlement, and a purchase at the ex-date open does not (line 42), and that events come before the open, then sales, then purchases (line 122). It does not specify the order of accrual and crediting, or the fate of the entitlement when shares are sold at the ex-date open. Accrual is placed before crediting and before trades: a sale at the ex-date open keeps the entitlement, a purchase does not create it, and a payout whose payment session equals the ex-date (scenario proxy +0) is credited at the open of that session. Cash credited at the open funds purchases at the same open, as sale proceeds do (line 122). Alternatives: crediting before accrual would not credit a +0 payout at the ex-date open, contrary to line 42; accrual after trades would give the entitlement to the buyer and remove it from the seller. Consequence: a +0 payout becomes cash in the same session and immediately funds purchases; this is a calculation simplification, not a model of a brokerage account.

2. Pending order at a split. The protocol requires consistent units for positions and unfilled orders, retention of a fractional residual until sale, and an integer new target (line 120), but does not say how to rescale an order quantity. The order is multiplied by the split ratio without rounding again, while target_qty and held_qty stay in decision-time units. Alternatives: rounding the order after rescaling, which would change the decision, or recomputing the target at the post-split price, which would use information after the decision close (line 120 forbids sizing by a not-yet-known Open). Consequence: after a split with a ratio below 1 (BIL, 2017, ratio 0.5) the position and the order may be fractional; the residual is sold by an order for its quantity, and the purchase of a fractional order is rounded down (item 10).

3. Two-session delay. The protocol permits execution one extra session later (line 173) and forbids sizing by a not-yet-known Open (line 120); it does not say what stays fixed between decision and execution. The order quantity is fixed at the close of the decision date and changes only through a split, while fill is computed from actual cash at the open of the execution session, after that session's credits and sales. Alternatives: recomputing the size at the close of the next session (this would shift the decision information, not the execution) or computing fill from cash at the decision date. Consequence: with lag = 2, prices and payouts have time to change the account, so a purchase may be filled incompletely or leave cash unused.

4. No trade threshold. The protocol introduces no minimum trade size or dead band, and a new economic rule requires a protocol version (line 126). Any nonzero difference between target and actual quantity creates an order. Alternative: a threshold in dollars or as a share of NAV so as not to trade small residuals; it would be another parameter with no basis in the protocol. Consequence: after position drift, orders for a single share with cost c are possible; turnover and costs are higher than they would be with a threshold.

5. Proxy scenario for the payment date. For payouts with no known date, the protocol provides a sensitivity of +0 and +30 calendar days, and actual dates take precedence (lines 42 and 174); it does not say how to recompute. The engine recomputes the crediting date only for rows with payable_basis `proxy_ex_plus_10_calendar_days`: the first XNYS session no earlier than ex-date + k calendar days. `actual` rows are not changed. No new vintage is created for this, and the basis is recorded as `proxy_ex_plus_<k>_calendar_days`. Alternatives: a separate vintage for each k (would require new reconciliation, QA and a separate decision under D020) or applying k to all payouts (contradicts line 174). Departure from the specification text: the specification (sections 2 and 5) refers to the first session of the common calendar, while the code uses the XNYS calendar, because normalize.py writes XNYS dates and a payment date may fall after the last bar; on the approved vintage the common calendar and XNYS coincide (4,869 of 4,869 sessions), so the result is the same. Consequence: the vintage has one proxy row (BIL, ex-date 2008-03-03), so sensitivity to k affects at most one payout, and its contribution was not measured; for k = 10 the recomputed date equals the vintage `payable_date`, which the loader checks.

6. Cash and quantities in float64. The protocol gives a tolerance of cash >= -1e-8 USD for the numerical residual (line 124) and does not specify how cash is stored. Cash and quantities are stored in float64 without rounding to cents; a value below -1e-8 after purchases raises `ValueError`. Alternatives: rounding every movement to the cent (needs a rounding rule for costs and receivables that the protocol lacks) or Decimal (a rounding rule is still needed and it adds no conformance to the protocol). Consequence: cash and NAV contain fractions of a cent, identities are checked with tolerances, and manual reconciliations are compared with abs=1e-9.

7. Guard for the closed period. The period from 2023-01-01 is closed until N6 (lines 137 and 143), but the protocol does not describe the prohibition mechanism. A run is rejected if end_session is later than 2022-12-30 (the last session of 2022) or the execution session of any decision is later than end_session. Alternatives: checking only decision dates (a decision on 2022-12-30 would execute at the 2023-01-03 open, that is, on closed data) or relying on run discipline. Consequence: by default the last decision falls on 2022-11-30; the guard applies to the run and does not restrict file reads, because the loader reads the vintage through 2026-10-05; only a separate decision can lift it.

8. Fill coefficient in the result. The protocol defines the coefficient itself (line 122) but does not require the engine to report it. `ledger.execute_orders` returns the pair (trades, buy_fill), decisions.csv stores buy_fill, and without purchases the value is 1.0. Alternative: reconstructing the coefficient from the purchased and ordered quantity, but after floor it cannot be recovered exactly. Consequence: buy_fill = 1.0 in a decision row does not mean that purchases occurred; their presence is read from orders.csv and trades.csv.

9. Default decision dates. The protocol defines t as the last regular session of the month (line 50) but says nothing about an incomplete month at the end of the window or a decision whose execution session falls outside the window. By default a decision is taken on the last XNYS session of each calendar month in the window, if its execution session (index + lag) exists and is no later than end_session. Alternatives: the last data session of each month (a window ending mid-month would produce a false monthly date) or decisions without execution in the window (an order would remain pending after end_session). Departure from the specification text: section 4 refers to the last session of the month in the common calendar, while the code uses XNYS; on the approved vintage the calendars coincide (4,869 of 4,869), so the result is the same. Consequence: for a window ending 2022-12-30 the last decision falls on 2022-11-30; changing the window shifts decisions only at its edges.

10. Fractional purchase after a split. After a split an order may be fractional, for example 456.5, while the protocol requires whole shares (line 120) and purchase of floor(fill × requested_quantity) with the cancellation reason retained (line 122). floor(fill × q) is bought. The reason `insufficient_cash` is set only if fewer than floor(q) shares were bought; otherwise status `partial` gets the reason `fractional_quantity`. Alternatives: writing `insufficient_cash` everywhere (this attributes a cash shortfall where cash was available) or buying the fractional part as well (contradicts whole shares). Consequence: an order with a fractional part receives `partial` even when fill = 1, and the position stays below target by the fractional part. The required amount R is computed from the full fractional q, so when cash lies between the actual spending and R, fill falls below 1 and a purchase of another ticker may shrink by one share. R computed from the full q follows the specification (section 6) literally; the effect was not measured.

11. Status on invariant violation. The specification (section 8) calls such a run `failed`. The engine ends it with status `invariants_failed`: the result is frozen in `data/runs/<run_id>` for inspection, and the names of failed checks go to quality_warnings. This follows N1, where audit writes `quality_failed`: a defect found by a check differs from an exception in code, and the files are needed for inspection. Alternatives: status `failed` without freezing the result (nothing to inspect) or a nonzero command-line exit code (audit also returns 0 on `quality_failed`). Consequence: `simulate` exits with code 0 on `invariants_failed` and prints the run directory, so a consumer must check `passed` in invariants.json or the status in the journal; code that looks only for `failed` in the journal would miss such a run.

12. Numbering in invariant_rotation. In the specification (section 10), w_i = (1 + (m + i) mod 3) / Σ, where i is the ticker's index in alphabetical order; the base of the index is not stated. Adopted: i is counted from zero over tickers in ASCII order, m = 12 × year + month of the decision date. Alternative: one-based numbering shifts the rotation by one step. Consequence: the choice does not affect the invariant checks, and the rotation still produces trades every month; using the month number within the year instead of m gives the same weights, because 12 is divisible by 3. The test `test_invariant_rotation_weights` fixes the numbering, and changing the formula requires a new provider version (currently 1, recorded in config.json).

13. Scope of the payable contract check. The specification (section 2) states the contract for rows with dividend > 0 and does not say which rows of the file to check. The loader checks the contract and numeric columns only on sessions of the common calendar, that is, from 2007-05-30: earlier rows in the SPY, EFA and other ticker files are not used by the engine. Alternative: checking all file rows, in which case a defect in history before the common start, which does not affect the account, would stop a run. Consequence: the loader asserts nothing about rows before 2007-05-30.

14. Overlapping decisions and provider weights. The specification forbids a new decision before the previous one executes (section 6) and requires weights for all ten tickers (section 3). The engine rejects a decision list before the loop starts if a decision index is less than the previous index plus lag, even when the previous decision created no orders. Weights are accepted only if the keys equal the market tickers, the values are int or float (not bool), are finite and not less than 0, and `math.fsum` does not exceed 1 + 1e-12. Alternatives: rejecting only when orders are pending (would miss an overlap in which there happen to be no orders) and zero weight for unnamed tickers (would hide a provider error). Consequence: monthly decisions with lag <= 2 do not overlap, and the check fires on explicit date lists; test providers name all tickers, with zero weight written explicitly; `np.float32` and `np.int64` values are rejected, while `np.float64` passes.

15. Rejections before a Run exists. The protocol requires every run, including failed ones, to be registered (section 14, line 192, and [experiments/README.md](experiments/README.md)). The journal is opened in `run_simulation`, Scenario validates the grid when it is created, and argparse parses arguments earlier. Therefore EXPERIMENT_LOG.jsonl does not receive Scenario values outside the grid, an unknown provider name (`KeyError` from Python; the command line restricts the choice through `choices`) or argument-parsing errors. Rejections inside a Run are journaled: the closed period, a different or external vintage, and input-data errors as `failed`, and invariant violations as `invariants_failed`. Alternative: recording every call, including argument typos, for which the grid check would have to move inside Run. Consequence: an attempt to run a scenario outside the grid (for example `--cost 0.003`) leaves no trace in the journal and creates no results, while an attempt to reach the closed period leaves a trace.

16. Units and format of output files. The specification (section 7) lists the output file columns without units or signs. order_qty is written with a sign (minus means a sale) in execution-session units, after rescaling for a split; filled_qty is the executed quantity, unsigned; target_qty, held_qty and close refer to the decision moment, in pre-split units. Floating-point numbers are written with the format %.10g, and config.json and invariants.json as canonical JSON without loss of precision. Alternatives: full repr precision (rounding tails remain in the low digits, and differences between files are harder to read) or order_qty in decision units (filled_qty and trades are in execution units, and rows would stop reconciling). Consequence: prices and amounts in the CSV are not equal to in-memory values bit for bit, and identities recomputed from the CSV hold to about 1e-4 USD; the invariant checks work on unrounded values with a tolerance of 1e-6.

17. Names of invariant checks. The specification (section 8) lists seven checks without names. In invariants.json they are named cash_non_negative, nav_identity, cash_flow, split_quantity_only, receivable_conservation, execution_timing and costs; each has the fields `passed` and `detail`. Alongside are the overall flag `passed` and the lists split_events ([ticker, session]) and proxy_payouts ([ticker, ex-date, payment session] for each accrued payout on a proxy row, as recomputed for the scenario). Alternative: free-text report that cannot be checked by key. Consequence: the N2 report and consumers refer to these keys, and renaming a check changes invariants.json and these references.
